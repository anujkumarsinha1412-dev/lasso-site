When a shopper asks an AI what the best vitamin-C serum under $40 is, the answer is not pulled from a ranking. It is assembled on the spot, out of sources the engine retrieved and decided to trust. Understanding that assembly step is the whole foundation of Answer Engine Optimization.

## Two different memory systems

Every AI answer draws on some mix of two systems, and the difference between them decides what a marketer can actually influence.

Parametric knowledge is what the model absorbed during training. It is frozen at a cutoff date and changes only when the model is retrained. If your brand was not prominent in the training data, there is no quick fix that inserts you here.

Retrieval-augmented answers are built live. The engine searches the web, reads current sources, and synthesizes a response. Perplexity, search-enabled ChatGPT, and Google AI Overviews all work this way, and they pick up new content within days. This is the layer where a brand can realistically move, and it is the right layer to measure.

The practical rule follows from that split. You cannot quickly change what a model knows. You can change what it finds. AEO is the discipline of changing what it finds.

## Each engine looks somewhere different

Cross-engine testing and citation analysis point to fairly consistent differences in source preference.

Perplexity leans on Reddit and other community discussion, and favours recent content. A brand with no authentic Reddit presence is fighting uphill there.

Google AI Overviews correlate strongly with YouTube, where transcripts make video content machine-readable, and draw on Google's shopping and review infrastructure, including Amazon listings.

ChatGPT draws noticeably on Wikipedia, comparison and "best of" content, and for shopping queries on whatever its search layer surfaces. Amazon blocks ChatGPT's crawler, which is why an Amazon presence helps in AI Overviews but does comparatively little in ChatGPT.

The implication is uncomfortable for anyone hoping for one tactic. A single-channel strategy produces single-engine visibility. The brands that appear everywhere have built signals across all of the source families, not one of them.

## What the research says moves answers

The Princeton GEO study (Aggarwal et al., KDD 2024) is still the most-cited controlled experiment in this area. Three findings matter most.

Citation tactics moved lower-ranked sites up substantially. A fifth-ranked site gained roughly 115% in AI visibility. The largest gains went to challengers rather than to sites already at the top.

Format mattered nearly as much as presence. Verbatim-quotable statements lifted visibility by 41%, against 28% for standard attributed citations. Engines prefer content they can lift whole, which means self-contained, clearly sourced, quotable blocks.

Fluency and statistics helped. Clear, confident, data-backed writing was cited more often than hedged marketing copy.

Set that alongside the trust-signal picture. Review depth and sentiment feed "best X" recommendations directly, and the FTC's Consumer Review Rule makes fake and AI-generated reviews explicitly illegal. So the durable play is accelerating real signals rather than manufacturing false ones. That constraint is also an advantage, because it is the reason the gains are hard to copy.

## The fragile monopoly of famous brands

AI engines recommend recognised brands by default. That default is a conditional monopoly, and the condition is that nothing distinguishes the alternatives. Give the engine a reason to name someone else, whether a well-cited comparison, a dense review base, or an active community thread, and the default breaks.

For challenger consumer brands this is the most asymmetric opportunity since early search. The incumbent advantage is real but thin, and most incumbents are not defending it yet.

## What this means in practice

If you run a consumer brand, three questions determine your AI visibility right now.

When AI engines answer your category's buying questions, are you named at all? Which sources feed those answers, and do you exist on them? And can a machine parse your site, meaning schema, consistent entities and quotable content, or does it hit a wall of unstructured brand copy?

You can check the first question yourself in about ten minutes. Open ChatGPT, ask your category's buying question five times in fresh sessions, and write down who gets named.
